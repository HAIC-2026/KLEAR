## Caveats

- Vintages differ. ACS facts are 2020-2024 5-year estimates that describe the whole five-year period, while block weights and district lines use the 2020 census. Population that moved within a tract after 2020 is not reflected in the apportionment weights.
- Population bases differ. Hawaiʻi draws its districts on the permanent-resident base, which leaves out non-resident military members, their dependents and non-resident students. The ACS counts everyone living in an area, so district ACS totals are not the reapportionment population.
- The margin of error for population 65 and over is a root-sum-of-squares approximation over the 12 age and sex cells of table B01001, following Census Bureau guidance. It is not a published margin of error, and it ignores correlation between the cells.
- Income-eligible estimates sum published ACS income-to-poverty bins and combine their margins of error as a root sum of squares, which ignores correlation between the bins and overstates the margin when many bins are zero. They meet the income test only: assets, age, immigration status and household rules are not measured. Where a program limit falls between published cuts, such as SNAP's 130 percent between the cuts at 125 and 150 percent, both cuts are stored and nothing is interpolated. The ACS measures income against national poverty thresholds, which sit below Hawaiʻi's poverty guidelines, so counts near a program limit may run low.
- Not yet checked: per-district 2020 populations against the Reapportionment Commission's published district tables. This is a planned check, and this report makes no claim about it.
- Ranges. Every apportioned (tier A) and modeled (tier M) fact with a value carries value_low and value_high. For counts moved from ZIP codes or tracts, the low counts only units wholly inside the district, and the high counts every unit touching it plus the most a suppressed unit touching it could hide. The IRS 99999 amount has no known location: it is assigned to districts by 2020 population, the high includes only the district's population share of it, so its true district share is not inside the guaranteed range. A CMS county residual is placed in that county's suppressed ZIP codes and counted whole in the high of every district they touch, but the residual location is assumed, not published. For PLACES, district ranges average the tract confidence limits with the same population weights as the estimate, so they assume tract errors move together and are wider than a formal confidence interval, and a suppressed tract counts as 0 in the low and 100 percent in the high.
- Apportioned district values (IRS, CMS, vouchers, CHAS) use 2020 block population as weights. The apportionment accuracy table above is the error to expect from such numbers where no direct district estimate exists.
