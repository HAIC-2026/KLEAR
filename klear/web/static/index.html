      How it works

      The pictures below are screenshots, not buttons. To try it yourself, use the search at the top of the page.

      1. Pick a district

        Search by district number, town, neighborhood, island, ZIP code, or street address, or tap the district on the map. When a place spans districts, KLEAR lists the House and Senate districts separately and shows how its residents split.

      2. Ask a question

        Ask in plain words about residents, housing, income, or programs, or start from a suggested question.

      3. Check the numbers

        Each figure shows its margin of error or range, the state value for comparison, and the source and survey years behind it.

      Data and sources

      Every figure KLEAR shows comes from one of these public sources. Survey years and district coverage vary by measure.

      Public sources in the KLEAR data store
      SourcePublisherYears

      The source list appears here once the data store is built.

      • Where a figure comes from ZIP code or census tract data estimated for a district, KLEAR shows a range, not a single number.
      • Modeled health and needs estimates from CDC PLACES are shown as ranges too. How KLEAR estimates these ranges.
      • Missing data stays missing. KLEAR does not fill gaps with guesses.
      • Program links go to each program’s official page. KLEAR does not decide anyone’s eligibility.
      • For Hawaiʻi public data that is not published by district, such as crime, schools, energy or state budgets, try datatrove.ai (external site), a HAIC 2026 sponsor.
